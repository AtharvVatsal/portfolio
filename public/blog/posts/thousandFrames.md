# A Thousand Frames, Four Years, and a Life Worth Remembering

*Somewhere between the first day of college and the last few months, four years quietly passed by. And I don't think I was quite ready for them to.*

I still remember arriving at VIT Vellore for the first time. Coming from a small town tucked away in the mountains, where the entire population was roughly equivalent to the number of people I was about to share a university with, it was quite a transition. Everything felt bigger, unfamiliar and different. The weather, the people, the campus, the opportunities and the sheer scale of everything around me.

![The First View I Saw When I Entered.](/blog/college/vit-arrival.jpg)

I came here to pursue an engineering degree. Like most people joining college, I had a rough idea of what the next four years might look like. A degree, a few friends, some projects, a little exploration and eventually, a job. I had plans, interests and things I wanted to try. What I didn't realise was just how much these four years would end up giving me, and how much of myself I would discover along the way.

Looking back, I find it almost amusing how quickly everything happened.

Somewhere between attending classes, submitting assignments, preparing for examinations and figuring out what I actually wanted to do with my life, college became so much more than academics.

I got to work on projects I had always wanted to build. I explored technologies, experimented with ideas, collaborated with people who were incredibly talented and learnt things that I know will stay with me long after the degree is out of the picture. I got opportunities to work on things beyond the classroom, take up gigs, meet professionals and interact with some exceptionally bright minds. I had professors who were generous with their knowledge, pushed me to think differently and made learning genuinely worthwhile.

And then there was photography.

I came to college with an interest in photography and, over the years, got the opportunity to turn that interest into something much bigger. From photographing just the stunt show in Riviera 2024 to whole of Rivera in 2025 to returning for Rivera 2026, from figuring out how college festival teams worked to eventually becoming a Student Organizer in the Press & Media team of graVITas'26, the journey was something I could hardly have imagined on my first day here.

![Riviera 2025 -SAE Stunt SHow.](/blog/college/riviera-2024.jpg)

![Sunidhi Chauhan - Riviera 2026](/blog/college/riviera-2026.jxl)

I got to stand behind the camera, capture moments, work with wonderful people and eventually become part of the teams creating those moments in the first place. There were events, shoots, late nights, deadlines, backstage chaos and memories that will probably find their way into my photographs for years to come.

![The graVITas 2026 Press & Media team](/blog/college/gravitas-team.jpg)

I think that's one of the things I will cherish most about college. It gave me the freedom to explore different sides of myself. I wasn't just a Computer Science student. I got to be an engineer, a photographer, a project enthusiast, a team member, an organiser and, most importantly, someone who was constantly discovering something new.

But if there is one thing that makes these four years truly special, it isn't a project, a position, an event or an achievement.

It's the people.

The people you meet in classrooms, the friends you make while figuring out assignments, the teammates who stay up with you to get something done, the people you randomly run into on campus, and even those you only exchange a few words with every day. You don't always realise how much these little interactions mean until you start thinking about a time when they won't be part of your everyday routine anymore.

!(/blog/college/friends.jpg)

And I know I am going to miss that.

I am going to miss the crowd, the noise, the constant movement and the liveliness of this campus. The random conversations, the terrible jokes that somehow never stop being funny, the unplanned plans, the last-minute gatherings and the feeling that there is always something happening somewhere.

!(/blog/college/first-yr.jpg)

I am even going to miss the things I probably complained about the most.

Those late nights before examinations, the last-minute assignment submissions, the endless project reviews, the stress of CATs and FATs, quizzes that somehow always arrive at the wrong time, and the collective panic when a deadline is approaching. The days when everyone is asking everyone else what is coming in the examination, or when an entire group is trying to figure out something that could probably have been understood much earlier.

At the time, these things feel exhausting. You count the days until examinations are over, wait for the semester to end and look forward to the holidays. You wish there were fewer assignments, fewer classes and fewer responsibilities.

And then one day, you realise you would give quite a lot to experience some of those days again.

I will miss Vellore, too. Coming from the mountains, adjusting to what often felt like an entirely different climate was an experience of its own. There were days when I missed home more than I could explain. The familiar surroundings, the mountains, the comfort of being back with family and everything that made home feel like home.

Yet, somehow, this place became another home. Not necessarily because of its weather or geography, but because of the people, the experiences and the memories attached to it.

![A quiet VIT campus evening around the SJT building.](/blog/college/sjt.jpg)

I don't think I ever fully understood how much could happen in four years. There were moments of excitement, uncertainty, stress, happiness and everything in between. Some days were incredibly productive, some were completely ordinary, and some were days I would rather not repeat. But together, they made up an experience that I wouldn't exchange for anything else.

And that brings me to something I have been thinking about quite a lot lately.

I genuinely feel fulfilled with the college life I have had. Not because everything went perfectly, or because I did everything I wanted to do, but because I gave myself the opportunity to explore, participate, experiment, fail, learn and experience things outside my comfort zone. There are certainly a few things I would do differently. A few opportunities I wish I had explored sooner and a few moments I would love to revisit. But looking at the bigger picture, I wouldn't change much.

!(/blog/college/first-year.jpg)

If I were given the opportunity to live these four years again, I would gladly take it.

Perhaps that is the best thing I can say about my time here.

### To everyone who is still living their college years

Whether you're reading this as someone about to enter college, someone already halfway through their degree, or someone who graduated years ago, there is something I really want to say.

Enjoy it. Seriously, enjoy it.

Don't spend all your time waiting for the next semester, the next holiday, the next examination to get over or the day you finally graduate. Don't get so caught up in your plans for the future that you forget to experience what is happening around you right now.

Go for that event. Join that team. Pick up that camera, learn that instrument, build that project, participate in that competition or attend that random gathering you weren't initially planning to go to. Talk to people. Make friends outside your usual circle. Take up opportunities even when you aren't entirely sure whether you're ready for them. Explore interests that have absolutely nothing to do with your degree.

Make memories that don't necessarily add anything to your resume.

Take those photographs. Record those random videos. Save those silly conversations. Attend the events, even if you don't know many people there. Spend time with your friends without always having a reason to. And every once in a while, allow yourself to do absolutely nothing.

You don't have to turn every single day into an achievement. Not every experience needs to be productive, and not every opportunity has to lead somewhere. Sometimes, the best memories come from the most ordinary days.

Because here's something I can assure you: **college goes by much faster than you think.**

One moment you're figuring out your first semester, trying to understand the campus and getting used to an entirely new environment. Before you know it, you're attending your final few classes, looking at old photographs and wondering where all those semesters went.

The people you see every day will eventually go their separate ways. The familiar faces in the corridors will become occasional appearances on social media. The campus that once felt like an ordinary part of your routine will become a place you revisit through photographs and memories. Even the things you once complained about will find a special place in your heart.

And perhaps, years from now, you won't remember every lecture, every examination or every assignment. But you'll remember the people you sat with, the conversations you had, the things you built, the events you attended and the feeling of being surrounded by thousands of people who were all figuring out their own lives, just like you.

### A thousand frames, and a lifetime of memories

Photography has taught me that sometimes, you only understand the importance of a moment after it has passed. You take a photograph without knowing how much you will eventually associate with it. An ordinary evening, a crowded event, a familiar face or a random moment with friends can become something incredibly valuable with time.

I think college has been much the same for me.

Over these four years, I have collected thousands of frames, countless experiences and more memories than I could ever fit into a single blog. Some of those memories are captured on my camera, while others exist only in conversations, friendships and moments that I will probably never be able to recreate.

As I approach the end of this chapter, I know I am going to miss almost everything about it. The people, the campus, the crowd, the chaos, the opportunities, the good days and even some of the not-so-good ones.

I came here from a small town in the mountains, carrying my expectations and a whole lot of curiosity. I am leaving with a degree, a camera full of memories, experiences I will always be grateful for, friendships I hope will last a lifetime and a version of myself that is very different from the one who first walked through these gates.

I came here to study engineering. I leave having learnt a little about life, a lot about people and so much about myself.

Four years might sound like a long time when you're just beginning. Trust me, it isn't.

So, wherever you are in your college journey, make the most of it. Don't wait until your final semester to realise how wonderful the little things were.

Because someday, you will look back at what feels like an ordinary day today and wish you could live it just one more time.

And when that day comes, I hope you have a thousand frames of your own to look back at.

**Here's to four years, a thousand frames and a life worth remembering.**
